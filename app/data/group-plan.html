3. Group content system + problem research

Reader and course problem

The intended reader is a Newark resident or NJIT student trying to understand what is listed for a council meeting. The problem to test is whether a short overview with related agenda items and direct sources is easier to use than opening separate records.

The proposed course direction is educational-content publishing: import public material, prepare a factual briefing, review it and publish an approved result. The proposed pilot is the meeting scheduled for November 5, 2026 (meeting 302232) and explicitly selected linked agenda records, subject to both teammates’ agreement. It does not cover the complete agenda or current council calendar. The group brief permits this workflow.

Shared workflow

  1. Extract: import a bounded selection of official meeting and agenda metadata.
  2. Transform: normalize IDs and dates, remove duplicate imports, retain source URLs and content versions, then split records into passages.
  3. Load and retrieve: save the records and passages in SQLite and find material related to the selected meeting or topic.
  4. Prepare: create an editable briefing with a citation beside each factual statement. Missing evidence stays visible.
  5. Review: the other teammate checks the statements against the referenced source versions and either approves the draft or requests changes.
  6. Publish: display the approved briefing, its sources, review state and checked date on a public page.

The shared deliverable adds briefing preparation, review and publication to the existing Newark retrieval work. The individual chatbot remains separate project evidence.

Proposed choice: B, with A retained as the comparison. The useful product is the reviewed briefing. AI may help prepare it, while a teammate remains responsible for checking its claims.

Proposed architecture and data

Reuse Python/FastAPI, server-rendered pages and SQLite. Keep the source manifest, versioned passages, retrieval index and citation mappings. Add briefing drafts, author/reviewer IDs, revision history, review decisions and publication state. Account data and credentials stay outside the retrieval corpus.

The intended state flow is draft → in review → approved → published, with requested changes returning a draft for revision. Editing an approved briefing requires another review. Record the source versions used; changed or unavailable sources need a visible review flag.

The first hosting choice is the existing $6/month server, subject to resource checks before Build. The Plan creates no new server or paid AI usage. Any generation uses the existing private server-side configuration and bounded request limits.
